## Business context

The model knows your data tools, but not your business. Without context, it guesses what "revenue" means, which quarter "this quarter" is, and which numbers matter. Business context tells it what a new analyst learns in their first week.

### What to include

- **What the business does.** What you sell, to whom, and how you make money, in a line or two.
- **Who reads the dashboards, and what they decide.** "Sales managers check pipeline weekly to choose which deals to push." The model picks metrics and detail for that reader.
- **Your terms.** The words your users type, especially ones you use differently from most, or that have synonyms: "Logos are customer accounts."
- **How key metrics are calculated, and what good looks like.** Say which direction is good when it isn't obvious, and give thresholds: "Churn above 2% a month is a concern."
- **Which tool answers which term.** "ARR comes from `get_subscriptions`, active subscriptions only." This is how the model turns a word into the right query.
- **Calendar and units.** Fiscal year, week start, time zone, currency, and rounding.
- **How the business is split.** The dimensions people compare: regions, segments, product lines.
- **Defaults.** The time range, comparison and lead metrics to use when a prompt doesn't say.

### What to leave out

| Leave out                                            | Instead                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Anything about one user: their name, team, or region | Nothing. Dashboards get shared, so your tools scope each viewer's data with [`context`](/docs/dashboards/connect-data#use-the-context). |
| What one tool returns: its fields and units          | The tool's `description`                                                                                                                |
| What one dashboard should show                       | The `prompt` of `create` or `edit`                                                                                                      |
| Current numbers, such as last month's revenue        | Your tools. Numbers in text go stale.                                                                                                   |
| Layout, colors, and components                       | The library's `promptOptions`                                                                                                           |
| Secrets and personal data                            | Nowhere. Instructions go to the model on every create and edit.                                                                         |
| History, mission, and marketing copy                 | Nowhere. It doesn't change what a dashboard shows.                                                                                      |

The first row matters most. If the context says "Maya is on team East", Maya's dashboard gets built for team East, and so does the copy she shares with Jordan.

### How to write it

- **Write facts, not requests.** "The fiscal year starts on February 1" works better than "Be careful with dates."
- **One fact per line**, in plain words, so it's easy to review and change.
- **Use your users' words**, the ones they'll type in prompts.
- **Be specific.** Numbers, names, and thresholds, not "the important metrics".
- **Only define metrics your tools can produce.** If you define margin but no tool returns cost, the model tries to show a number it can't compute.
- **Keep it short.** A page of facts, not your wiki. Long text buries the facts that matter.
- **Test it with vague prompts**, such as "How's this quarter going?" or "Show me churn". When the model picks the wrong metric or period, add the fact it was missing.

### Example

```text title="Business context"
We sell project-management software to construction firms, on annual and monthly plans.
Dashboards are read by sales managers (pipeline, forecasts) and customer success managers (renewals, account health).

Terms:
- Account: a paying customer company. "Logo" means the same.
- Pipeline: open deals in the Qualified, Demo, Proposal, and Negotiation stages, from get_deals.
- ARR: annual recurring revenue from active subscriptions, from get_subscriptions. A monthly plan counts as 12 times its price.
- Churn: accounts whose subscription ended in the period, as a share of accounts at its start. Lower is better; above 2% a month is a concern.
- Health: the 0–100 score from get_account_health. Below 40 is at risk.

Calendar and units:
- The fiscal year starts on February 1. "This quarter" means the fiscal quarter.
- Times are US Eastern. Weeks start on Monday.
- Amounts are in USD. Show them in thousands, such as $12.4k.

We compare by region (NA, EMEA, APAC) and segment (SMB under 50 seats, Mid-market 50–500, Enterprise over 500).

Unless asked otherwise, show the last 90 days against the 90 days before, and lead with ARR and churn.
```

On November 24, a sales manager asks "How's this quarter going?":

- **Without this context**, the model guesses: calendar Q4 (October 1 to today), the total of every deal amount, one daily line.
- **With it**, it shows fiscal Q4 (November 1 to today): new ARR and churn against the quarter before, and pipeline by stage, split by region.

### Provide it

Pass the context as `instructions` to `defineDashboard`. It's read on every `create` and `edit`.

```ts title="lib/openui.ts"
import { businessContext } from "./business-context";

export const dashboards = openui.defineDashboard({
  library: spec,
  model: "anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5",
  tools: dataTools,
  instructions: businessContext,
});
```

## Groups

Groups decide who can open a dashboard. OpenUI matches a group [share](/docs/dashboards/sharing) against the `groupIds` you pass, and never stores who's in a group.

### What to include

- **The units people already share with**: teams, departments, the whole company.
- **Every level above the user**, so a share with a department reaches its teams. Maya, on team East in Sales at Acme, gets `team:t_81`, `dept:d_7`, and `company:c_42`.
- **One group for everyone**, such as `company:<companyId>`. There's no built-in share for everyone.

### What to leave out

- **Permissions, feature flags, and roles nobody shares with.** They use up the 10.
- **Groups meant to limit data.** A share opens the dashboard; your tools decide which data it shows.

### Ids

- Use stable ids prefixed by kind, such as `team:t_81`, never names, which change.
- A user can be in up to 10 groups, and each id is up to 64 characters.
- If your app serves many companies, ids must be unique across them: `team:sales` at two companies is one group to OpenUI. Use database ids or prefix them with the company, and never give every company the same `everyone` group. The same goes for user ids.

### Provide them

```ts title="lib/groups.ts"
export function groupsFor(user: User): string[] {
  return [
    `company:${user.companyId}`,
    ...user.departmentIds.toSorted().map((id) => `dept:${id}`),
    ...user.teamIds.toSorted().map((id) => `team:${id}`),
  ].slice(0, 10); // a fixed order, so the same groups are cut every time
}
```

Pass the result as `groupIds` everywhere you pass `userId`: on the frontend token and in every server call's `context`. A change reaches the browser with the user's next token, within 15 minutes.
