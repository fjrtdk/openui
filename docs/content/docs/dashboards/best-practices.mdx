## Business context

The model knows your data tools, but not your business. Business context tells it what your terms mean, which period "this quarter" is, and which numbers matter.

### What to include

- **What the business does**, and who reads the dashboards.
- **Your terms**, especially ones you use differently from most: "Logos are customer accounts."
- **Key metrics**: how each is calculated, which tool answers it, and what good looks like, such as "Churn above 2% a month is a concern."
- **Calendar and units**: fiscal year, time zone, currency, and rounding.
- **Defaults**: the time range and comparison to use when a prompt doesn't say.

### What to leave out

- **Anything about one user**, such as their name or team. Dashboards get shared, so scope each viewer's data in your tools with [`context`](/docs/dashboards/connect-data#use-the-context).
- **What one tool returns.** Put it in the tool's `description`.
- **Current numbers.** They go stale; your tools return them.
- **Secrets and personal data.**

### How to write it

- **Facts, one per line**, in your users' words: "The fiscal year starts on February 1", not "Be careful with dates."
- **Only define metrics your tools can produce.** Otherwise the model tries to show a number it can't compute.
- **Test it with vague prompts**, such as "How's this quarter going?". When the model picks the wrong metric or period, add the fact it was missing.

### Provide it

Pass it as `instructions` to [`defineDashboard`](/docs/dashboards/define-dashboards). It's sent with every `create` and `edit`.

## Your agent

Your agent, the model your users chat with, decides when to make a dashboard and writes its prompt. The dashboard model never sees the conversation: only that prompt, your `instructions`, and your data tools. Start from the system prompt in [Add to your agent](/docs/dashboards/add-to-your-agent#instruct-the-agent), and make sure it covers the points below.

### What to tell it

- **What dashboards can show.** List your data tools, one line each, so it turns down requests they can't answer instead of making an empty dashboard.
- **When to make one**: when the user wants to track, compare, or explore numbers. Otherwise, reply in text.
- **Edit, don't recreate.** A change to an open dashboard goes to `edit_dashboard` with its id. Make a new one only when asked.
- **It can't see the data.** It can say what a dashboard shows, but not the numbers in it.
- **Keep replies short.** The user watches the dashboard build, so one or two sentences are enough.

### How it should write the prompt

The prompt is the dashboard model's whole brief, so it has to stand on its own:

- **Resolve the conversation.** Replace "that account" or "like before" with the actual names, filters, and periods.
- **Start with the question behind it**, such as "Is the team on track for the quarter?", so the model knows what to lead with.
- **Name what to show**: metrics, breakdowns, time range, comparisons, and the filters people can change.
- **Write "the viewer's team", not a person's name**, unless the user means one team by name. The dashboard gets shared, and your tools scope it to whoever opens it.
- **No numbers.** The dashboard loads them through your tools. Include data only when your tools can't fetch it, such as a list the user pasted.
- **Leave out your business context.** `instructions` already carries it.
- **Give it a short, specific title**, such as "Q4 pipeline by region". Users see it in their lists.

| Weak                                     | Better                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Make the sales dashboard we discussed." | "Is the viewer's team on track this fiscal quarter? Lead with open pipeline against last quarter, then pipeline by stage and the 10 largest open deals. Add region and segment filters." |

### Edits

- **One change per edit**, described against what's there: "Add a table of the 10 largest open deals below the stage chart."
- **For a dashboard from an earlier chat**, call `get_dashboard` first to see its layout and whether `isEditable` is true. If it isn't, the user can only view it, so make a new one instead.
